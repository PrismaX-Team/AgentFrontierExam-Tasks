Risk-controlled selective gating for cross-session motor-imagery BCI

A motor-imagery brain-computer interface (BCI) decodes imagined movements from EEG. A decoder calibrated on one day works worse on a later day, by an amount that varies unpredictably between people. A safe interface therefore acts only on trials it is likely to decode correctly and abstains on the rest. Your task is a gating policy that keeps the error rate among the trials it acts on at or below the target risk r = 0.20 on a later recording session, while acting on as many trials as possible.

Protein-Pocket-Conditioned 3D Ligand Design with Verifiable Synthesis Routes

Design and rank synthesizable three-dimensional ligands for three fixed protein pockets: trypsin_3ptb, abl1_1iep, and brd4_3mxf. Each target supplies a prepared receptor, a 22 Angstrom cubic docking box, public molecular-property bounds, an explicit required-interaction group, one task-authored amidation SMARTS, one target-oriented core, and a finite allowlist of compatible building blocks. Every submitted candidate must use the canonical evaluation SMILES produced by the frozen RDKit protocol and must be reachable by exactly one replayed reaction step from target-allowed starting materials. The public one-step product universe contains 20 trypsin, 35 ABL1, and 20 BRD4 products (75 total); this pilot tests selection under a scarcer docking budget rather than unrestricted molecular generation.
